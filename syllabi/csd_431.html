This course provides undergraduate students with a comprehensive introduction to audiology and hearing science. Students will explore the profession of audiology, the anatomy and physiology of the auditory system, the basic principles of acoustics and hearing perception, and the nature and impact of hearing disorders. The course introduces major types of hearing loss, including conductive, sensorineural, and mixed hearing loss, and examines their effects on communication and daily functioning. Students will also learn the scientific principles, administration, interpretation, and clinical application of foundational audiology assessment procedures such as pure-tone audiometry.

By the end of this course, you will achieve the following:

  1. Define the fundamental terminology used in audiology, hearing science, and hearing disorders.
  2. Recall the basic anatomy and physiology of the auditory-vestibular systems.
  3. Describe the physical properties of sound, including frequency, intensity, and their perceptual correlates.
  4. Explain the typical processes of hearing and auditory perception.
  5. Identify the roles and responsibilities of audiologists across clinical, educational, and research settings.
  6. Differentiate among conductive, sensorineural, mixed, and central auditory hearing disorders.
  7. Classify hearing disorders according to type, degree, onset, time course, and laterality.
  8. Interpret basic audiograms and recognize common patterns of hearing loss.
  9. Distinguish between air-conduction and bone-conduction findings and their clinical implications.
  10. Explain the principles, purposes, and clinical applications of basic audiology assessment procedures such as pure-tone audiometry.
  11. Apply foundational audiology knowledge to interpret basic hearing assessment results.
  12. Evaluate the impact of hearing disorders on communication, educational participation, social interaction, and quality of life.
  13. Apply acquired knowledge to make informed decisions regarding future educational and professional opportunities in audiology and related fields.
  14. Assess personal strengths, areas for growth, and readiness for continued learning in audiology.

Academic integrity is a serious matter, and any deviations from appropriate behavior will be dealt with strongly. At the discretion of the professor, situations of concern may be dealt with as a scholastic matter or a disciplinary matter.
